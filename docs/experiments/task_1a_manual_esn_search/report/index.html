Task 1-a · Manual teaching · Nominal-only ESN search

Nominal success does not guarantee robust multi-demonstration control

An echo-state network trained on ten human-guided reaches can pass from its taught starting posture, yet lose much of that success under starting offsets and force disturbances. This report explains a controlled parameter search, the resulting five-arm comparison, and what the evidence does—and does not—establish.

01 / General background

Learning a motion, then closing the loop

Teaching by demonstration replaces a hand-designed motion with examples. A human guides an arm from a starting posture to a target, and a learned system should reproduce the task while coping with deviations. Recording more examples may supply useful variation, but it also changes the learning problem: the same target can be approached along different transient trajectories.

An echo-state network (ESN) is a recurrent dynamical model. Its fixed random reservoir transforms inputs and their history into an internal state; a linear output layer is trained by ridge regression. Reservoir size, recurrence, leakage, input scaling and regularization affect the resulting dynamics. This experiment searches those parameters, with a fixed random seed, rather than training all recurrent weights.

The ESN here receives measured joint positions and velocities, through a frozen input transform, and predicts the next desired joint position. It is a reference generator, not a torque controller. A separate feedback tracker turns desired motion into torque, subject to safety limits. During training the inputs come from demonstrations; during execution they come from the simulated arm. Consequently, fitting demonstrated next positions is not sufficient to establish that the closed loop will converge after an unfamiliar state.

Replay provides a useful baseline: a fixed recorded trajectory is followed by the same tracker. It has no ESN and does not adapt the reference to state, although its tracking controller still uses feedback. Good replay performance shows that the demonstration and tracker can accomplish many of these cases; it is not evidence of generalization to new targets.

02 / Experiment background, aim and scope

Does tuning on ten demonstrations change the comparison?

A preceding study compared one and ten manually recorded trajectories using six configurations inherited from a scripted-teacher study. Ten demonstrations did not consistently improve performance. That left an important qualification: inherited parameters might favor the earlier task formulation rather than the manual bank. The present follow-up searches ESN parameters on the all-ten training arm itself.

Primary question. At parameters selected for the nominal success of a model trained on all ten demonstrations, how do that model, single-demonstration models, duplicate controls, synthetic augmentation and replay compare when the starting state or external force is perturbed?

Only M10 nominal outcomes enter optimization. Singletons, copies, augmentation and perturbed cases are evaluated after selection freezes. This protects the specified optimizer from perturbation feedback. The perturbation cases are not newly drawn: they were used in earlier research, so this is a development comparison with cases withheld from this search, not a fresh confirmatory test.

The scope is one planar arm, one target, one bank of ten demonstrations, one reservoir seed and two fixed trackers. It contains no new recordings, separately optimized singleton models, multiple-seed validation, hardware trials or adaptive retuning after robustness outcomes become known.

03 / Methodology

A matched comparison with explicit controls

Robot, teaching and task

The gravity-free planar arm has two revolute joints, with link lengths 0.30 and 0.25 m. Its taught reset posture is [0.2, 1.2] rad and its endpoint target is [0.10, 0.45] m. Ten accepted mouse-guided recordings share that reset and target but retain natural differences in transient motion and initial fluctuation. They were acquired at 50 Hz, reconstructed from actual timestamps, smoothed while preserving the first sample, and placed on the common 100 Hz training/control grid. Full recordings contribute to training; they are not cropped to a fixed move window.

The learned readout predicts absolute next joint position from reservoir state and an explicit bias. Each episode has equal total loss weight despite different duration. For K episodes, each row of episode i has weight 400/Li, and the ridge penalty used by the solver is Kα0. Equivalently, the objective uses mean squared loss per episode plus α0/400 times the squared weight norm. The explicit bias is penalized consistently. This makes exact duplication a control for numerical equivalence, rather than a way to dilute regularization. The input transform is inherited and fixed; it is not estimated from the other nine teachers when a singleton is trained.

Nominal score = number of successful tracker runs / 2 ∈ {0, 0.5, 1}

The three distinct parameter points with the highest nominal scores are selected, with ties resolved by earlier trial number. No timing, trajectory-quality or perturbed score breaks ties. “Highest nominal scores” therefore does not mean “the three best” controllers.

Frozen evaluation and success definition

Every learned model attempts all 65 cases from fresh resets under both trackers. The panel contains one nominal case, 20 small-posture offsets (0.05 rad scale), 20 large-posture offsets (0.10 rad scale), four force directions, and 20 combined posture/force cases. These are a fixed panel, not 65 independent samples from a known real-world distribution.

A force case applies a 12 N endpoint pulse for 0.2 s, triggered after the measured arm holds the target predicate continuously for 0.5 s. Its actual timestamp is recorded separately for every run. Thus matched force direction does not imply matched wall-clock pulse time. Failing to reach the trigger condition is a failure to complete the prescribed test, not evidence of surviving the disturbance.

Success requires completing the 30 s active horizon and satisfying every configured criterion: final uninterrupted hold of at least 1 s within 1 cm of the target with every joint at or below 0.05 rad/s; valid generated reference; the required force trigger and post-pulse hold where applicable; and effort admissibility. The 6 rad/s joint-speed abort and torque limits [10, 5] N·m remain active. An early abort counts as a failure, retains its partial trajectory, and does not suppress subsequent cases. A favorable final endpoint alone cannot override a failed criterion.

Results are paired within configuration, tracker, parent and scenario class. For M10−S, the same M10 result is compared with each of the ten singleton parents. M10 is counted once in per-arm totals. Parent-wise differences, medians, ranges and improved/worsened/tied counts describe heterogeneity; they are not independent replications of M10 and are not confidence intervals.

Copies do not supply new information

Across 3,900 matched S/R10 scenario/tracker pairs, there are 0 verdict disagreements. This is consistent with the count-scaled ridge construction: repeating an episode does not change the intended regularized objective. This statement concerns verdicts; it does not claim bitwise equality of every learned trajectory or establish that adding different demonstrations cannot matter.

Replay and trackers remain important controls

All replay parents pass 63/65 PD cases and 64/65 computed-torque cases at each selected configuration. The recorded pooled totals are 3,810 successful replay runs out of 3,900, versus 7,044 learned successes out of 12,090. Those pooled numbers are bookkeeping, not an estimate of the M10 effect: there are ten S, R10 and C10 models but one M10 per configuration.

Strong replay performance makes a blanket explanation based on untrackable demonstrations implausible. It does not identify the cause of each learned failure. The tracker comparison is also substantive: at T4, PD permits many synthetic recoveries that computed torque does not. Holding gains and filters fixed isolates the declared search; it does not establish their insensitivity.

07 / Discussion and limitations

Separate what was measured from possible mechanisms

Supported conclusion: selecting for M10 nominal feasibility is insufficient to secure a robust advantage over one-teacher models in this fixed panel. The result addresses the predecessor's parameter-inheritance qualification by searching on M10, but it is not a controlled before/after estimate of tuning's benefit: the configuration panel, seed and fixed filter policy differ from that earlier comparison.

Selection is intentionally weak on robustness. The objective has only three values. Seventy candidates tie at the maximum, and chronology decides which three are retained. The study finds nominally feasible parameters; it does not rank those seventy by robustness, smoothness, effort or timing. We cannot infer that no unselected candidate would perform better under perturbations.

A plausible mechanism, not a demonstrated cause: teachers share endpoints but trace different state histories. A common readout must combine those histories, and perturbed feedback may take the reservoir outside its demonstrated state distribution. Ridge regularization and reservoir dynamics could then shape unhelpful attractors or sensitivity to initial conditions. These explanations require additional diagnostics or controlled ablations. Different outputs at similar instantaneous postures are not proof of contradictory labels, because the ESN carries memory. The present data do not establish a causal link between any one searched parameter and the observed robustness.

The inference is conditional on M10 selection. S and C10 were evaluated at parameters selected for M10, not separately optimized for their own objectives. Their good results in some strata are meaningful baselines under common parameters; they do not identify either arm's attainable optimum. Likewise, augmentation changes more than episode count: R10 controls duplication, but C10 tests only one frozen noise construction.

  • Only ten demonstrations of one target and one fixed reservoir seed were used. No between-operator or between-seed uncertainty is estimated.
  • The two nominal tracker outcomes are deterministic evaluations, not repeated Bernoulli trials. Parent ranges and scenario fractions are descriptive, not population confidence intervals.
  • The 64 perturbed cases were withheld from Optuna but already known to the broader research process. No new confirmatory case set was drawn.
  • Paired M10 contrasts reuse a single M10 model within each stratum; treating ten contrasts as ten independently trained M10 replicates would overstate evidence.
  • A strict 1 cm final hold and the generated-reference, force and safety criteria shape success. Passing does not imply equal motion quality, and near misses should not be silently recoded.
  • Tracker/filter insensitivity was not tested. Their values were fixed by design; the observed tracker differences caution against assuming interchangeability.
  • The audited computation and presentation are reproducible from stored artifacts. They do not validate model fidelity to a physical robot, nor authorize hardware deployment.

08 / Conclusion

Nominal tuning resolves feasibility, not robustness

At the three configurations selected by the approved nominal-only M10 search, all-ten training does not reliably outperform one-teacher learning. It has a negative median paired effect in five of six strata, with a modest positive exception at T1/computed torque. Duplication leaves verdicts unchanged, while synthetic augmentation is strongly dependent on configuration and parent. Replay remains a strong baseline.

The practical lesson is to retain matched singleton, duplication, synthetic and replay controls when studying multi-demonstration learning, and to evaluate perturbed closed-loop behaviour separately from the objective used for selection. Any future decision to tune robustness, vary seeds, inspect reservoir states or introduce fresh scenarios would be a new protocol. This completed comparison alone does not choose or authorize that next study.
